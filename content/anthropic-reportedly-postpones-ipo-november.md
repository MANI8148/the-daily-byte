Anthropic is delaying its planned IPO from October to November 2026, according to a report from The Decoder. The company reportedly wants stronger Q3 financial results before going public, even as investors reportedly line up behind a valuation approaching $2 trillion.

The delay follows a similar move by OpenAI, which also reportedly pushed back its own public listing plans. The pattern suggests that going public in 2026 is harder than AI startups originally expected.

## What the delay signals

Pushing an IPO by one month is not unusual on its own. Companies routinely shift dates to align with market windows or stronger earnings. But the timing here is notable: October was already late in the year for a 2026 listing, and November leaves even less time to build momentum before year-end.

A one-month delay also implies that whatever Anthropic saw in its earlier financial projections was not enough. The stated goal — presenting strong Q3 results — suggests the company is prioritizing perception over speed. Going public is a permanent step. A botched debut can haunt a stock price for years.

## The $2 trillion question

Investors are reportedly eyeing a valuation of roughly $2 trillion. To put that in perspective, that figure would make Anthropic one of the most valuable companies on Earth — comparable to the market caps of Apple, Microsoft, or Saudi Aramco at their peaks.

For context, here is what that valuation looks like broken down by hypothetical share counts:

```
# What a $2T valuation implies per share
valuation = 2_000_000_000_000  # $2 trillion

for shares_millions in [1_000, 2_000, 5_000]:
    price = valuation / (shares_millions * 1_000_000)
    print(f"{shares_millions:,}M shares -> ${price:,.2f} per share")

# Output:
# 1,000M shares -> $2,000.00 per share
# 2,000M shares -> $1,000.00 per share
# 5,000M shares -> $400.00 per share
```

These are illustrative numbers. Anthropic has not disclosed its share count or IPO pricing range publicly. But the exercise highlights the scale investors are debating: any reasonable share count still yields an enormous per-share price.

Whether the market would actually agree with that valuation at the open is a separate question. AI stocks have seen sharp corrections this year after initial enthusiasm.

## OpenAI set the precedent

Anthropic is not the only major AI lab reconsidering its timeline. OpenAI also reportedly postponed its IPO plans. Both companies had been expected to go public in 2026.

The reasons appear similar: strong revenue growth but also rising costs, uncertain profitability timelines, and a public market that has grown more selective about AI investments. After Openai's delay, some analysts assumed Anthropic would follow suit. This report confirms that pattern.

The two companies also face different pressure. OpenAI has a more established consumer brand via ChatGPT. Anthropic's Claude competes on safety and reasoning claims, which may be harder to translate into investor enthusiasm at a $2 trillion price tag.

## Rising infrastructure costs

The original report cited rising infrastructure costs as a key concern. Training and running frontier AI models requires massive compute. Cloud GPU costs, data center construction, and energy contracts run into billions of dollars annually.

For a company that has not yet gone public, these costs create a tricky balancing act. Spending heavily on infrastructure signals confidence but also burns cash fast. Investors want to see that revenue growth can eventually outpace these expenses. Q3 results are reportedly the proof point Anthropic is waiting for.

This is not unique to Anthropic. Every major AI lab faces the same tension between capability and cost. The difference is that Anthropic has not yet shown whether its revenue trajectory can support a $2 trillion claim.

## What Q3 results are on the line

Anthropic reportedly told investors it needs more time to assemble a convincing Q3 picture. Revenue, burn rate, and enterprise adoption figures would all factor into whether the IPO opens well.

Enterprise contracts — deals with companies that pay for Claude API access or Claude Enterprise — are particularly important. Consumer subscriptions alone likely cannot justify a $2 trillion valuation. Large-scale B2B revenue would.

If Q3 numbers come in strong, November could still work. If they disappoint, the delay may extend further. Markets do not forgive weak debuts.

## What this means for investors

For retail investors, the takeaway is patience. A delayed IPO is not necessarily a red flag, but it does shift the risk window. Every month without public trading means less liquidity and more uncertainty about where shares will actually price.

Institutional investors reportedly remain interested, but they will be scrutinizing Q3 closely. A single strong quarter could be enough. Two weak ones could kill the listing entirely.
