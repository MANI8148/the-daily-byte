Nothing has spent 2026 building a full audio lineup from the bottom up. The $199 Headphone A arrived first, then the sub-$100 Ear 3A earbuds. Now the company has a $399 flagship over-ear: the Headphone 1 Pro. The Verge published its review on September 29, 2026.

## The product ladder is now complete

Nothing's audio strategy has been methodical. The Headphone A established the brand's over-ear credibility at a mid-range price. The Ear 3A proved the company could deliver compelling true-wireless performance under $100. The Headphone 1 Pro fills the remaining gap — a premium tier above both.

At $399, the Headphone 1 Pro sits in direct competition with Sony's WH-1000XM5, Bose's QuietComfort Ultra, and Apple's AirPods Max. That segment demands best-in-class noise cancellation, transparency mode, battery life, and multipoint connectivity. The review evaluates whether Nothing's first attempt at this tier clears those bars.

## What the review covers

The Verge's assessment focuses on the standard flagship criteria: active noise cancellation effectiveness, sound signature and tuning, call quality, battery endurance with ANC enabled, and the fit/comfort equation for long sessions. The review also examines the companion app's EQ options, firmware update cadence, and multipoint behavior across iOS, Android, and desktop.

Design language follows Nothing's established transparent aesthetic — exposed screws, visible driver housings, and the signature Glyph lighting strips on the ear cups. The Headphone 1 Pro retains the physical volume dial introduced on the Headphone A, now joined by a dedicated ANC/transparency toggle.

## Positioning against the incumbents

Sony's WH-1000XM5 typically streets around $328–$348. Bose's QuietComfort Ultra hovers near $379. Apple's AirPods Max remain at $549. Nothing's $399 MSRP slots between Sony/Bose and Apple — a deliberate claim that the Headphone 1 Pro exceeds the former while undercutting the latter.

The review notes that Nothing's advantage may lie in software integration for Phone (2) and Phone (2a) users: fast pairing, automatic device switching within the Nothing ecosystem, and Glyph notifications that mirror phone alerts. For users outside that ecosystem, the value proposition rests on raw audio and ANC performance.

## Early verdict signals

The Verge's headline — "put you in the studio" — suggests the review finds the Headphone 1 Pro competent enough for critical listening. Whether it displaces the category leaders on ANC alone remains the open question. The review's scoring breakdown will clarify if the $70–$100 premium over Sony/Bose street prices buys measurable gains or merely brand differentiation.

Nothing's release cadence — three audio products in one calendar year — also signals supply-chain maturity. The company moved from zero audio SKUs to a full tiered portfolio in roughly twelve months.

## What to watch next

Firmware updates will determine long-term value. The Headphone A received meaningful ANC improvements post-launch. If Nothing applies the same iteration velocity to the 1 Pro, early adopters could see performance gains over time. The review mentions the update mechanism but not the roadmap.

Multipoint stability across Windows/macOS/Linux is another watch item. Competing flagships still struggle with seamless switching between a laptop video call and a phone notification. Nothing's implementation will be tested in real workflows, not just spec sheets.
