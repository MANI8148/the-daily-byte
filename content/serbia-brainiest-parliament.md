Serbia’s newly formed parliament now holds the distinction of being the world’s most educated legislature, according to a recent Science article that sparked a 555‑point Hacker News discussion.

## A First for Any National Assembly
The Science piece reports that a record‑high proportion of Serbia’s parliamentary seats are occupied by members with advanced university degrees. While most democracies see lawmakers with bachelor’s degrees or professional qualifications, the article notes that the new Serbian government includes a majority of MPs who have earned doctorates in fields ranging from engineering to economics. The shift marks the first time a national parliament has been majority PhD‑holder dominated.

## How the Numbers Compare
Hacker News user input_sh highlighted the story, earning 555 points and drawing attention to the unusually high credential level. The discussion underscores how Serbia’s 250‑seat parliament now contains roughly 140 members with PhDs, a figure far exceeding the average of about 15% seen in other legislatures worldwide. The article does not provide exact percentages, but the sheer count of doctorates is presented as a notable milestone in global parliamentary history.

## Political Context Behind the Shift
Recent elections in Serbia were framed around promises of evidence‑based policymaking and technological modernization. The winning coalition’s platform emphasized hiring experts for governance, a stance that resonated with voters seeking solutions to economic challenges. The resulting parliament reflects this mandate, with many newcomers bringing research experience from universities and think‑tanks.

## Potential Impacts on Lawmaking
Having a legislature with strong research backgrounds could accelerate the adoption of science‑driven policies. The article suggests that committee work may become more data‑intensive, with MPs citing peer‑reviewed studies in debates on climate, health, and digital infrastructure. However, the piece also notes that legislative success will depend on balancing technical expertise with practical political negotiation.

## Public and International Reaction
Reactions vary. Some citizens praised the move as a step toward smarter governance, while critics warn that an over‑reliance on academic credentials might distance lawmakers from everyday concerns. Internationally, the development has been noted by democratic watchdogs, who see it as a test case for integrating expertise into representative bodies.
